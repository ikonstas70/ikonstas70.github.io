TCP/IP, Explained

Video series · 5 November 2026 · Ioannis Alexander Konstas

The Internet Protocol is designed for use in interconnected systems of packet-switched computer communication networks. It moves blocks of data, called datagrams, between hosts identified by fixed-length addresses, fragmenting and reassembling them when a network along the way carries only small packets. When it was standardized in the early 1980s, it was a Department of Defense standard: RFC 760, DoD Standard Internet Protocol; RFC 761, DoD Standard Transmission Control Protocol; and RFC 810, DoD Internet Host Table Specification.
