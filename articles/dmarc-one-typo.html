One Misspelled Word in DMARC

Articles · November 2026 · Ioannis (John) Konstas

A routine check of a real business domain found a DMARC record with one misspelled word. The business is not named here; the domain is shown as example.com and every other value is a documentation example. The findings were read from public DNS (Cloudflare 1.1.1.1 and Google 8.8.8.8 returned the same records) and nothing was changed.

What was found

_dmarc.example.com.  TXT  "v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; pct=100; rua=mailto:reports@example.net; sp=quarentine; aspf=r;"
  • p=quarantine, pct=100, rua= and aspf=r are valid.
  • sp=quarentine is not: the only valid values are none, quarantine and reject.
  • SPF on the same domain ends in ~all (soft fail), not -all.
  • The domain sends through five services: Microsoft 365, Proofpoint Essentials, Mailchimp, Postmark and Resend.

What the standard says about an invalid tag

RFC 7489, section 6.6.3, covers a record that "contains an 'sp' tag that is not valid". When the record also has a valid rua address, as this one does, the receiving server should act as if the record said p=none: report, but do not quarantine or reject. In plain words, a receiver that follows the text strictly can treat the whole policy as monitor-only.

What this does and does not mean

  • It does weaken protection against forged mail in the domain's name at any receiver that applies the rule strictly. Forged messages that should go to spam may be delivered.
  • It does signal a less carefully managed domain, and a valid, enforced DMARC policy is now required of bulk senders by Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft.
  • It does not, on its own, prove why genuine mail from the domain lands in spam. Receivers such as Gmail decide mainly on sender reputation, complaint rates and content. Large receivers may also ignore only the bad tag instead of the whole record. The typo is one contributing factor, not the whole explanation.

Why the business should care

  1. Lost sales. Newsletters, order confirmations and customer replies that land in spam are not read.
  2. Mailbox rules are enforced. Since February 2024 Gmail and Yahoo require bulk senders (over 5,000 messages a day to Gmail) to have SPF, DKIM and a valid DMARC record, one-click unsubscribe and a spam complaint rate below 0.3%. Microsoft began enforcing similar rules for high-volume senders to Outlook.com in May 2025.
  3. The brand can be forged. A weak policy makes fake invoices, payment-change requests and "order problem" emails in the company's name easier to deliver. The FBI reports business email compromise losses in the billions of dollars each year.
  4. Reputation is shared. Marketing, receipts and staff mail share one domain reputation; complaints about bulk mail lower delivery for everything.
  5. Customer trust. Real mail in spam, or fake mail in the inbox, damages the brand either way.
  6. The fix is small. A few DNS edits, minutes of work, no cost, fully reversible.

The corrections

  1. DMARC (required). Change sp=quarentine to sp=quarantine: 
    v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; pct=100; sp=quarantine; aspf=r; rua=mailto:reports@example.net
  2. SPF. Change ~all to -all, after the DMARC reports confirm that no legitimate source fails SPF for the domain.
  3. Microsoft 365 DKIM. Confirm that signing is enabled in the Defender portal (Email authentication settings > DKIM). Published keys alone do not prove signing.
  4. Every bulk or app sender. Confirm that DKIM is verified for the domain in each service (Mailchimp, Postmark, Resend).
  5. Google Postmaster Tools. Register the domain to see its reputation, spam rate and DMARC pass rate at Gmail.
  6. Separate bulk mail to a subdomain (for example news.example.com) so marketing reputation cannot drag down business mail.
  7. Optional hardening: DNSSEC, MTA-STS, TLS-RPT and CAA.

Verify from the outside

dig +short TXT _dmarc.example.com @8.8.8.8
dig +short TXT example.com @8.8.8.8

Sources

  • Google: Email sender guidelines

One letter turned an enforced policy into a questionable one. Check DMARC from the outside, spell every tag exactly, and read the reports.
