Technical Report: Trimix Breathing Gas for Deep Technical Diving

Technical Report · September 2026 · Ioannis Konstas

Author: Ioannis Konstas

Technical Diver · Rebreather Specialist · Hellenic Navy Veteran

This technical specification details the physics, respiratory mechanics, and operational protocols of ternary breathing gas mixtures ($O_2\text{--}N_2\text{--}He$) for operations exceeding standard compressed air and nitrox envelopes.

1. Introduction

Trimix is a specialized breathing mixture composed of oxygen ($O_2$), nitrogen ($N_2$), and helium ($He$), engineered to support human life and performance at depths where conventional atmospheric air or enriched air nitrox (EANx) becomes physiologically hazardous or lethal. At ambient pressures exceeding 4 to 5 atmospheres absolute (ATA), air exposes divers to severe nitrogen narcosis, Central Nervous System (CNS) oxygen toxicity, and elevated gas density that induces dangerous carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) retention.

The primary mandate of Trimix is fourfold: reduce nitrogen narcosis to a manageable cognitive baseline, limit oxygen partial pressure ($P\text{O}_2$) within strict non-toxic windows, lower overall gas density to reduce the work of breathing (WOB), and preserve cognitive and respiratory stability during deep, extended-range, and overhead operations.

2. Gas Composition and Physiological Roles

Each constituent gas in a Trimix blend performs an exact physiological function governed by the gas laws (Dalton's, Henry's, and Graham's laws):

Oxygen ($O_2$)

Metabolic Life Support: Drives cellular respiration. Fraction ($F\text{O}_2$) is strictly regulated so that partial pressure never exceeds 1.4 ATA at working depth to prevent Paul Bert (CNS) toxicity convulsions.

Nitrogen ($N_2$)

HPNS Buffer: Heavily narcotic at depth, but deliberately retained in small fractions (typically 20–40%) to counteract the tremors and neural hyperexcitability of High-Pressure Nervous Syndrome.

Helium ($He$)

Density & Narcosis Reducer: An inert, light noble gas with near-zero narcotic potency at technical depths. Lowers mixture density to ensure smooth laminar airway flow and prevent $CO_2$ buildup.

3. Categorization of Trimix Formulations

Trimix blends are classified by their oxygen and helium fractions (standard notation: Tx O2/He, e.g., Tx 18/45):

Classification $F\text{O}_2$ Range Target Depth Operational Protocol
Normoxic Trimix $18\% \le F\text{O}_2 \le 21\%$ 40 m – 60 m Breathable at the surface ($P\text{O}_2 \ge 0.18\text{ ATA}$); no travel gas required during descent.
Hypoxic Trimix $F\text{O}_2 < 18\%$ (e.g., Tx 10/70) > 60 m – 100 m+ Lethal at surface. Induces instant hypoxia; requires separate travel gas to descend to switch depth.
Helitrox (TriOx) $F\text{O}_2 \ge 24\%$ 30 m – 45 m Nitrox base enriched with 15–35% helium to reduce narcosis on mid-range profiles.

4. Depth Limitations and Oxygen Partial Pressure ($P\text{O}_2$)

The operational ceiling and floor for any breathing mix are bounded by Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures:

\[ P\text{O}_2 = F\text{O}_2 \times P_{\text{ambient}} = F\text{O}_2 \times \left( \frac{\text{Depth (meters)}}{10} + 1 \right) \]

Technical diving standards enforce two strict operational limits:

  • Working Phase ($P\text{O}_2 = 1.2\text{ -- }1.4\text{ ATA}$): Maintained during descent and active bottom work to account for physical exertion, thermal stress, and unpredictable spikes.
  • Decompression Phase ($P\text{O}_2 \le 1.6\text{ ATA}$): Permitted only during resting, stationary decompression stops to maximize inert gas off-gassing gradients.

For a normoxic mix like Tx 21/35, the Maximum Operating Depth (MOD) at $1.4\text{ ATA}$ is calculated as:

\[ \text{MOD} = \left( \frac{1.4}{0.21} - 1 \right) \times 10 = (6.666 - 1) \times 10 \approx 56.6\text{ meters} \]

Descending beyond this on a 21% blend elevates $P\text{O}_2$ into the seizure threshold. For deeper exposures (e.g., 90 meters / 10 ATA), a hypoxic blend like Tx 12/65 is mandatory ($P\text{O}_2 = 0.12 \times 10 = 1.2\text{ ATA}$).

5. Decompression and Pure Oxygen Acceleration

During ascent, divers transition to hyperoxic decompression gases (typically EANx 50 and 100% pure $O_2$) to create a massive inert gas partial pressure gradient between dissolved tissue tensions and alveolar gas (the "oxygen window").

The 6-Meter Rule: Pure oxygen ($F\text{O}_2 = 1.0$) possesses an absolute Maximum Operating Depth of 6 meters (20 feet), where ambient pressure is exactly $1.6\text{ ATA}$ ($1.0 \times 1.6 = 1.6\text{ ATA}$). Breathing pure oxygen deeper than 6 meters dramatically increases the risk of acute CNS oxygen toxicity and submerged convulsions.

6. Physiological Dynamics & Gas Mechanics

A. Nitrogen Narcosis Mitigation & Equivalent Narcotic Depth (END)

Helium does not exhibit significant lipid solubility under biological technical pressures. By replacing nitrogen with helium, divers maintain an Equivalent Narcotic Depth (END) equivalent to 24–30 meters regardless of actual physical depth, ensuring rapid cognitive reaction times.

B. Gas Density & Work of Breathing (WOB)

At high ambient pressures, compressed gas becomes dense and viscous, transitioning airway flow from laminar to turbulent. This elevates the work of breathing, leading to hypoventilation and catastrophic carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) retention (hypercapnia). Technical guidelines mandate keeping gas density below $5.2\text{ g/L}$ (with an absolute ceiling of $6.2\text{ g/L}$). Helium ($0.1786\text{ g/L}$ at STP) drastically lowers mixture density compared to nitrogen ($1.2506\text{ g/L}$).

C. High-Pressure Nervous Syndrome (HPNS)

At depths beyond 120–150 meters, pure helium-oxygen mixtures (Heliox) cause severe neuromuscular disruption—tremors, dizziness, nausea, and myoclonic jerks—due to hydrostatic pressure altering cell membrane lipid bilayers. The inclusion of a small fraction of nitrogen ($N_2$) produces a mild narcotic counter-effect that stabilizes neural membranes and suppresses HPNS.

7. Operational Implementation & Gas Management

  • Rigorous Pre-Dive Gas Analysis: Every cylinder must be analyzed independently for $O_2$ and $He$ using calibrated electrochemical and thermal conductivity sensors. Cylinders are labeled with verified fractions, MOD, and date.
  • Decompression Algorithm Modeling: Profiles are calculated using Bühlmann ZHL-16C with conservative Gradient Factors (e.g., GF 30/70 or 40/85) to control microbubble formation.
  • Closed-Circuit Rebreather (CCR) Integration: CCR systems maintain a constant setpoint $P\text{O}_2$ (e.g., 1.2–1.3 ATA) dynamically by injecting pure oxygen against a Trimix diluent cylinder, optimizing gas consumption and decompression efficiency across all depths.

8. Conclusion

Trimix is the foundational life-support technology of deep technical diving. By systematically managing the partial pressure of oxygen, narcotic loading of nitrogen, and fluid density of the breathing gas through helium substitution, Trimix transforms extreme-depth operations from uncontrolled gambles into repeatable, engineered missions.

When combined with meticulous decompression scheduling, continuous sensor verification, and uncompromising psychological composure, Trimix provides divers with the physical and cognitive stamina required to explore the planet's deepest subterranean and oceanic frontiers safely.
