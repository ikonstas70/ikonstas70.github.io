What the Numbers Reveal

Chapter 1: One Login, the Whole Internet · 3 November 2026 · Ioannis Alexander Konstas

Snapshot note. The session in this chapter was captured on 17 October 2021 at 17:33. The figures describe the Internet as it was then; the method is unchanged. For a current session (3 November 2026), and how the numbers have moved in five years, see Chapter 2, Part 8.

1. This is a collector, and the counters prove it

Hurricane Electric sent 95.3 million messages in 34 weeks; the collector sent back 381,931. Over 241 days that is one message about every 55 seconds: keepalives and nothing else.

3. Correlated events are visible

AS 3356, 7018, 701 and 3549 have all been up for exactly 5d13h. Four of the largest networks did not fail independently in the same minute; something they share with the collector did: maintenance, a reboot, a common path. Spotting this is how operators correlate outages.

4. Prefix counts expose policy

Full feeds range from 845,477 to 880,273 prefixes. That 35,000-prefix spread is each network's filtering and export policy made visible: peering-rich networks and those that pass more-specific routes send more, while the large Tier-1 transit networks cluster tightly at 845k to 847k, consistent with strict filtering. You are reading routing policy from the outside.

5. The collector is healthy

Every Established neighbor shows TblVer 1142409035 with InQ and OutQ at 0: no backlog, all in sync.
