Summary and Key Findings

Chapter 2: Reading a Path · A field report from Route Views · 3 November 2026 · Ioannis Alexander Konstas

Chapter 1 read the summary of a route collector, from a snapshot taken on 17 October 2021. This chapter reads the routes themselves, in live sessions on 3 November 2026. Everything here comes from the public Route Views collector (AS 6447) and read-only commands. The output is real; the interpretation is the point.

The headline

Five years later, the Internet is far better protected

The same public collector, the same command, 17 October 2021 and 3 November 2026. In five years, the share of Internet routes that are cryptographically verified more than doubled, and invalid announcements fell by two thirds, while the routing table grew by more than 200,000 networks.

67.3% → 29.1%paths with no ROA at all

Scope and method

Vantage point: route-views.routeviews.org, the University of Oregon collector that peers with more than twenty full-table providers and filters nothing. Because it filters nothing, it shows the Internet as it actually is, including what a production router would have dropped.

What matters, in one sentence. A public collector shows not only where traffic goes, but why: business relationships, engineering intent, internal addressing and security gaps, for any network, without touching it.

Parts 2 to 5 take the findings one by one, with the output that proves each. Part 6 reads the router's own telemetry: CPU, memory, traffic, per-peer counters and access control. Part 7 investigates every multiple-origin conflict for leaks and possible hijacks, fairly. Part 8 turns it all around, and Part 9 proposes a peering design that is more secure and exposes far less. Part 8 uses a second session on the same collector to show the problems shrinking where operators act, and why that is an opportunity rather than a threat.
