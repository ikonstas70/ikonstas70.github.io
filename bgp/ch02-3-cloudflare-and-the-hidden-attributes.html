Cloudflare 1.1.1.1 and the Attributes That Leak

Chapter 2: Reading a Path · A field report from Route Views · 3 November 2026 · Ioannis Alexander Konstas

Sixteen paths, one ROA

route-views> show ip bgp 1.1.1.0/24
BGP routing table entry for 1.1.1.0/24, version 1509109845

      Community: 3356:3 3356:22 3356:100 3356:123 3356:575 3356:901 3356:2012

      Community: 7018:2500 7018:37171

Twelve of the sixteen paths are direct (X 13335). Four reach Cloudflare through a transit network, and three of those four go through Lumen. Hurricane Electric's path is 6939 3356 13335: from this vantage point HE reaches 1.1.1.0/24 through Lumen rather than directly.

Finding 4 · Medium

Cloudflare's traffic engineering is visible in Lumen's communities

Lumen supports action communities that its customers attach to ask for treatment downstream: 65000:AS do not announce to that AS, and 65001:AS to 65003:AS prepend one to three times toward it. Cloudflare's route carries:

CommunityRequested action
65003:4134Prepend three times toward AS 4134 (China Telecom)
65003:4837Prepend three times toward AS 4837 (China Unicom)
65003:58453Prepend three times toward AS 58453 (China Mobile International)
13335:100xxCloudflare's own tags, which differ per path and appear to identify the site that announced it

Why it matters: the request was meant for Lumen, but Lumen forwards the communities, so the whole Internet can read Cloudflare's policy toward specific networks. Your own action communities are just as public unless your provider strips them. Treat them as published.

Finding 5 · Medium

One legacy attribute reveals eleven internal routers

Each path carries atomic-aggregate and an AGGREGATOR attribute: the AS and router ID of the router that built the aggregate. Across the sixteen paths there are eleven different router IDs, all in private space: 10.34.0.16, 10.34.2.204, 10.34.3.248, 10.34.6.80, 10.34.7.24, 10.34.8.168, 10.34.9.112, 10.34.23.128, 10.34.24.72, 10.34.130.6, 10.34.130.16.

This is anycast made visible: the same prefix is originated at many sites, and each peer hears it from a different one. The attribute also discloses the internal addressing plan (10.34.0.0/16 for router IDs) and a lower bound on the number of sites peering with these networks.

Why it matters: AGGREGATOR is rarely useful today and is sent by default when you aggregate. If you summarize routes, check what your router puts in it. Internal router IDs are reconnaissance data you did not mean to publish.

The more-specific check

route-views> show ip bgp 1.1.1.0/24 longer-prefixes
     Network          Next Hop            Metric LocPrf Weight Path
V*   1.1.1.0/24       132.198.255.253                        0 1351 13335 i
V*                    217.192.89.50                          0 3303 3356 13335 i
...
V*>                   12.0.1.63                              0 7018 13335 i
V*                    202.232.0.2                            0 2497 13335 i

Only the /24 itself, sixteen paths, all RPKI Valid. No one is announcing a more-specific, and the ROA (1.1.1.0/24, maxLength 24, AS 13335, seen in Part 5) is tight. A /24 is also the longest IPv4 prefix most networks accept, so a sub-prefix hijack would be filtered almost everywhere. This is what good looks like, and it is the template for your own address space.

Defender's check. Run show ip bgp <your prefix> longer-prefixes on a public collector every day. Any line you did not announce is a leak or a hijack in progress.
