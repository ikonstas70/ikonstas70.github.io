The Session

Chapter 1: One Login, the Whole Internet · 3 November 2026 · Ioannis Alexander Konstas

Snapshot note. The session in this chapter was captured on 17 October 2021 at 17:33. The figures describe the Internet as it was then; the method is unchanged. For a current session (3 November 2026), and how the numbers have moved in five years, see Chapter 2, Part 8.
Most engineers configure BGP. Few ever look at the Internet through it. In this chapter we log in to a router that holds the routing table of more than twenty of the world's largest networks at once, read a single command's output line by line, and see how much it tells us: about the Internet, about the networks that run it, and about our own exposure.

The question

How big is the Internet's routing table right now? How fast does it change? Which carriers filter what? And what can anyone, attacker or defender, learn about a network without ever touching it?

One free, public login answers all of it.

Route Views

Route Views is a project of the University of Oregon. Its routers peer with networks around the world and collect their full routing tables, purely so engineers and researchers can see the Internet from many vantage points. It is public by design, and it is intended exactly for this.

Log in yourself

$ telnet route-views.routeviews.org
Username: rviews
route-views> show ip bgp summary

That is the entire setup. No account, no VPN, no API key.
