Special Topics in BGP: Reading the Internet's Routing Table

CCIE · Advanced · Ioannis Alexander Konstas

Most engineers configure BGP. Few ever look at the Internet through it.

This series logs in to the public route servers and looking glasses that carriers and exchanges operate for everyone, and reads the global routing table live: how traffic really reaches a network, which providers stand in between, how leaks and hijacks show up in plain sight, and how much a single public BGP view reveals. Every chapter is hands-on: the question, the exact session, the reading of its output, and what it means for your own network. It is written for engineers who already know the protocol.

From the first session

One public login and one command, show ip bgp summary, already show this:

916,896IPv4 prefixes in the global table
1.14 billionbest-path changes: the Internet's churn
17,810RPKI-invalid paths visible right now
3.6 GBof router memory to hold it all

Chapter 1 reads every one of those lines, the 57 neighbor sessions beneath them, and what that public view exposes about any network on the Internet. Start reading.
