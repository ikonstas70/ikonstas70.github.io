A ROA authorizes; it does not require an announcement. China Telecom (AS 4134) has ROAs for 1.1.4.0/22, 1.1.16.0/20 and 1.3.0.0/16, none of which appear in Hurricane Electric's listing. Authorized but unseen space is normal, and is worth monitoring: an announcement there from any other origin is invalid by design.

Finding 9 · Medium

The two caches do not agree

About 136,000 entries separate the caches. They may run different validator software, different trust anchors, or include IPv6 differently. Whatever the cause, the router's view of "valid" depends on which cache is up. With one cache reconnecting today, there were hours when the router may have had only one cache's view.

Why it matters: run at least two caches from different validator implementations, monitor their counts and serial numbers, and alert when they diverge or when one falls behind.

Finding 10 · High

Origin validation rests on a fragile, unauthenticated link

RTR (RFC 8210) here runs over plain TCP with no transport security and no TTL protection, and the connection history shows thousands of failures. Anyone who can reach that TCP session or impersonate a cache can feed the router a false ROA set: mark good routes invalid, or bad ones valid.

Why it matters: on your own routers, keep RTR on a protected management network, use SSH or TLS transport where the platform supports it, and decide deliberately what happens when every cache is unreachable. Most routers fall back to "NotFound" for everything, which silently turns origin validation off.

Your turn

On route-views.routeviews.org (username rviews). Start with terminal length 0 to avoid the --More-- prompt, and type commands exactly: IOS rejects abbreviations it cannot resolve, as how ip bgp shows.

The takeaway

Seven commands showed how BGP chooses among equal paths, how carriers label routes by region and relationship, how a content network's policy and internal addressing leak through attributes, how prepends and more-specifics shape traffic, and where RPKI is strong and where it is thin. None of it required access to any of those networks. Next: mapping a network from the outside.
