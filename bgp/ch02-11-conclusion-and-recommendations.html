Conclusion and Recommendations

Chapter 2: Reading a Path · A field report from Route Views · 3 November 2026 · Ioannis Alexander Konstas

Two read-only sessions on one public router, a few dozen commands, no access to any network we discussed. This part draws the conclusions and turns them into recommendations, in priority order.

Conclusions

#ConclusionEvidence
1The routing table is public, and it is a security report. Business relationships, traffic engineering, internal addressing and security gaps are readable by anyone, for any network.Communities (Finding 3), action communities (4), AGGREGATOR router IDs (5), prepends and more-specifics (7)
2RPKI works, and it is winning. Seven in ten IPv4 paths and more than eight in ten IPv6 paths are now valid; invalid announcements reach about one feed in fourteen.Parts 7 and 8: 70.8% and 83.0% valid today; invalid paths down 70% since October 2021
3Most "hijacks" are mistakes. Of 1,329 conflicting origins, none had the shape of a deliberate attack; the real conflicts were stale provider routes, cloud announcements and typing errors.Part 7: 64% authorized, 33% unjudgeable, 3.5% contained misconfiguration, no route leaks in the sample
4Origin validation has blind spots. It does not check paths, it is weakened by loose maxLength, and it depends on caches that can disagree or disappear.Findings 8, 9 and 10; the one-third of conflicts with no ROA at all
5The control plane is the soft target. A full-table router is CPU- and memory-bound, and the Internet throws billions of unsolicited packets at it.Part 6: CPU 36-100%, input-queue drops, 3.3 billion UDP packets to closed ports
6The fixes are cheap and verifiable. Almost every gap closes with minutes to days of work, and every fix can be checked from the same public login.Parts 8 and 9

Recommendations, in priority order

2Drop RPKI-invalid routes on every external sessionHijacks and leaks others did not stop (12)Hours
3Announce from an explicit export list only, with internal communities deletedLeaks and business disclosure (3, 4)Hours

For public collector operators

Route Views is a public good, and this chapter could not exist without it. Three small, respectful suggestions emerged from the telemetry: an idle timeout on public terminal lines (one session had been idle for over a year), restricting show users so the public cannot see each other's addresses, and a pointer to the BMP and archive feeds for heavy queries, to keep interactive load off a busy control plane.

Limits of this report

  • Two snapshots from one collector, five years apart: Chapter 1's capture of 17 October 2021 and the sessions of 3 November 2026. Peer mixes differ between them, so we compare shares, not counts.
  • RPKI states are this router's view; other validators may differ slightly at any moment.
  • The leak scan covered the paths collected here, not the full table.
  • No intent is attributed to any network. Conflicts were classified by evidence; where the evidence was insufficient, we said so.
Final word. BGP was built on trust, and the routing table still shows what that trust exposes. It also shows something more important: where networks publish what they own, validate what they accept and announce only what they should, the problems shrink, visibly, from one session to the next. Every network can make that change, and every change can be verified from a public login.
